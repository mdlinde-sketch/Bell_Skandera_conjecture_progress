## Exact symbolic reduction (no matrix inversion)

Let `q_j` be elementary symmetric coefficients of positive reciprocal roots, and `p_j` their Newton power sums. Define `M=(p_(i+j))_(0<=i,j<=2)`, `H2=det(M[0:2,0:2])`, `H3=det(M)`, `H4=det((p_(i+j))_(0<=i,j<=3))`. Put `v=(p3,p4,p5)` and `A=adj(M)`. The polynomial bordered-determinant identity (valid even if M is singular) is

    H4 = p6*H3 - v^T*A*v.

For fixed `q1..q4`, take `q5=z+t`, `q6=w+s`, where `z=L5(q4)`, `w=L6(z)`, and `t,s>=0`. Newton's identities give `p5=p5_0+5t`, `p6=p6_0+6q1*t-6s`. Hence the following is an **exact polynomial identity**:

    H4(z+t,w+s) = H4(z,w) + D*t -25*H2*t^2 -6*H3*s,
    D = 6*q1*H3 -10*(A[0,2]*p3 + A[1,2]*p4 + H2*p5_0).

For a real positive-root polynomial, the moment Hankel matrices are PSD, so `H2,H3>=0`. Newton's lower chord gives `q5>=z` and `q6>=L6(q5)>=L6(z)=w`. Consequently, if `H4(z,w)<0` and `D<0`, then `H4(q5,q6)<0` for **all** admissible `q5,q6`, without computing an upper bound for `q5`, a vertex, or any quadratic interval maximum. This is a **finite case-dependent monotonicity result**, not a universal sign theorem.
